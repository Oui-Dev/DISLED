# Board F1B5ABCD5A97BD15E339E339

Second v4 board (pcb 1.0.0, silkscreen DISLED Rev 1.0), the final
prototype: the touch thresholds of `firmware/src/app/app_config.c` come
from it. Tried on 2026-10-24 and 2026-10-25 on the Windows desktop PC,
plugged straight into it for the calibration.

Enclosure 1.0.0 (V2), closed. Touch electrode: a copper ring of 28.5 mm
outside and 17.5 mm inside diameter (the first board had 30/20 in
enclosure 0.1.0); the light sensor looks out through a 3 × 1.6 mm window.

## USB and app

- Release 0.3.0 flashed with the wchisp 0.2.3 of PlatformIO (SHA-256
  checked, verify OK). Serial number = chip UID, as above.
- The desktop app first showed "No lamp connected": a Corsair HID device
  of this PC (`1B1C:1B2E`) takes 15 s to answer each string request, on
  each of its 3 interfaces, so a full HID enumeration took 45 s, while the
  lamp answers in 8 ms. The app and `disled-factory` now look only at the
  lamp's vendor ID (4531fa4).
- `disled-factory --no-flash` on release 0.3.0: baseline 2103 at rest; the
  touch step passed on a light touch of 29 ‰ (the press threshold was 28
  then, 60 since firmware 0.3.1).

## Touch

TouchKey at rest: about 2100 counts (first board: about 2620). Values are
the drop below the baseline after the 20 ms average, in per mille, on the
earthed desktop PC.

| Situation | Drop (‰) |
|---|---|
| At rest, user at the desk (calibration) | Standard deviation about 5, single windows up to 25 |
| At rest, nobody near (3 h 42, below) | Standard deviation 0.7 |
| LED from off to 125 mA, any color and level | 0 ± 1 on average, same noise |
| Hand held 5 cm above the lid | About 3 |
| Hand held 2 cm above | 40 to 55 |
| Hand held 1 cm above | 60 to 80, peaks to 100 |
| Hand passing 1 cm above | Peaks of 33 to 58 |
| Light tap | 77 to 188 at the peak, least on the center (over the hole of the ring) |
| Firm tap | 120 to 200 |
| Finger held | About 170 |
| Phone lying on the lid | About 150 (246 at contact) |

- The lamp turned off at random with firmware 0.3.0: a hand hovering
  1 cm over the lid for 2 s read as a long press, and each pass as a tap.
  Firmware 0.3.1 raised the thresholds (press 28 → 60 ‰, rise above the
  floor 18 → 50, release 16 → 34, debounce kept at 40 ms): a finger lands
  faster than a hand nears the lid, so the rise above the floor sorts
  them. The reasoning is in the TouchKey note of `app_config.c`.
- Phone on the lid: a long press at 2 s, absorbed at 10 s (baseline down
  to 1793), and a new baseline once removed, as designed.
- Against the first board, a hovering hand reads about 3 times stronger
  and a touch about twice. The rings cannot be compared from this alone
  (different board, lead and ground); their copper areas are the same,
  397 and 393 mm².

### At rest for 3 h 42 (2026-10-25)

Release 0.3.1, desktop app (Diagnostics, telemetry at 1 s, CSV), nobody
near the lamp. 13,343 samples, none missing, from 5 s after a software
reset, so the diagnostics counters cover the whole test. A night
recording was lost to a power cut before it: the app keeps a recording in
memory until it is exported.

- The lamp stayed on, green, in automatic brightness: no short press (it
  would have stepped the color), no long press (it would have turned the
  lamp off). Touch state at rest in every sample. Counters: 0 re-baseline,
  0 lost touch reading, 0 fault, 0 watchdog reset, 0 USB suspend; stack
  never used: 1336 B.
- Baseline from 2104 to 2110 counts (2107 at the start, 2109 at the end);
  the hourly means stay within 0.5 count.
- Noise: standard deviation 0.7 ‰ of the 20 ms average (1.7 counts), 5.4
  counts on single readings, the same every hour. About 7 times quieter
  than the calibration, which had the user at the desk and the debug
  console streaming; the thresholds were set on the noisier case.
- Largest windows seen: 9 ‰ below the baseline, 5 ‰ above, each a single
  window (the telemetry shows one window in 50), far from the press
  (60 ‰) and under the proximity band (10 ‰). Four of them, 4 to 9 ‰,
  came about 81 s apart between minutes 94 and 99: an outside disturbance,
  without effect.

## Light sensor

- Found at boot, no I2C error.
- The lamp's own light reaches the sensor. LED steps of the calibration,
  enclosure closed, room at 3.1 lx with the lamp off, user at the desk:

| Lamp | LED current | Light added at the sensor |
|---|---|---|
| Warm white 10 % | 1.6 mA | 0.2 lx |
| Green 44 % (automatic) | 8.7 mA | 1.5 lx |
| Green 50 % | 11.6 mA | About 2 lx |
| Warm white 50 % | 26.1 mA | 2.8 lx |
| Red 100 % | 86.0 mA | 5.2 lx |
| Orange 100 % | 113.9 mA | 9.8 lx |
| Green 100 % | 63.0 mA | 10.5 lx |
| Warm white 100 % (current cap) | 125.0 mA | 12.7 lx |

- It follows the light of each color, not its current (red at 86 mA adds
  half of green at 63 mA): light coming back, not an electrical coupling.
  In automatic brightness the lamp takes it for room light: turned on at
  36 % in that room, it settled at 44 %. It cannot keep itself lit in the
  dark: at every level measured, its own light stays under half of the
  light the curve needs for that level.
- In the dark (3 h 30 of the test at rest), at its floor (1.5 %, green),
  the lamp read 0 count at the most sensitive setting (under 0.0084 lx),
  where the coupling above predicts about 2 counts: part of that light
  may come back from the surroundings (the user sat next to the lamp).
- At the end of the test at rest, the room lit up from 0 to 0.47 lx in
  about 8 s: the level followed from 1.5 to 8.8 % in about 5 s, on the
  curve (6.2 % at 0.25 lx for 6.0 from the curve, 8.8 % at 0.44 lx for
  8.9), moving by 0.6 % a second at most, and came back to the floor
  under 0.1 lx.
